Four lines of work, one standard of rigor

Each assignment combines what the problem calls for. An impact evaluation starts with good field research and is only worth doing if the results reach the people who decide.

Research

Sampling design, instruments and baseline.

We design the sample, write questionnaires and interview guides, train the team and supervise data collection. The baseline is the picture of the situation before the program starts.

  • Household surveys
  • Stratified sampling
  • Interviews and group discussions
  • Analysis of official data

Monitoring

Indicators and dashboards to adjust course early.

We start from the theory of change to define a few indicators that matter, set up the data collection routine and deliver dashboards the team uses.

  • Theory of change the expected path from the program's actions to its results
  • Indicator matrix
  • Interactive dashboards
  • Maps and network analysis

Evaluation

Causal inference to measure what actually changed.

We choose the design that the data and the program's timeline allow, and we state clearly what it can claim.

  • Difference-in-differences compares how participants changed with how a similar group changed
  • Matching compares participants and non-participants with similar profiles
  • Regression discontinuity compares those just above and just below the eligibility cutoff
  • Social return on investment estimates the social value generated for each real invested

Learning

Evidence that becomes decisions, together with the teams.

We share findings in workshops, executive summaries and policy briefs, and we train teams to read and produce their own numbers.

  • Feedback workshops presentation and discussion of results with the team
  • Executive summaries
  • Policy briefs
  • Training in data analysis

From kickoff to feedback

An evaluation goes through five stages. The work plan sets the timing of each one with the client.

  1. Kickoff

    Kickoff meeting, review of program documents and agreed evaluation questions.

  2. Design

    Strategy to isolate the program's effect, sampling and instruments, validated before going to the field.

  3. Fieldwork

    Data collection with a trained team, daily supervision and data quality control.

  4. Analysis

    Effect estimation, robustness checks and a record of the study's limitations.

  5. Feedback

    Presentation and discussion of results in a workshop with the program team, final report and executive summary with recommendations.

From the Semi-arid to the Amazon

The team works where programs happen: rural communities in the Semi-arid, family farming cooperatives in Maranhão and Pará, neighborhoods along the streams of Manaus. Knowing the territory changes the research question, the questionnaire and the reading of the results.

Who leads the work

For each assignment, Limber assembles a team of associate researchers in quantitative methods, qualitative research and field coordination.

Lucas Fernandes

Managing partner and technical lead

Economist, PhD candidate in Economics at the Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte, with a master's degree in Urban and Regional Planning from the Federal University of Minas Gerais. Research collaborator at the Rural Studies Laboratory and the Quantitative Methods Laboratory of the same university.

He has worked for more than ten years in monitoring, evaluation and applied research, from family farming and solidarity cooperatives to urban sanitation and energy transition programs. He studies the effects of wind farms and of the Garantia-Safra crop insurance program in the Semi-arid.
